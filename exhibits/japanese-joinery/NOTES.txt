SCOPE
Illustrative studies of documented mechanisms, with independent display dimensions.
No measured historic specimens, fabrication tolerances or structural certification.
The Kawai-inspired study is modern, and explicitly distinguished from traditional joints.

01 Koshikake aritsugi / Dovetailed lap
https://www.aisf.or.jp/~jaanus/deta/k/koshikakearitsugi.htm
280 × 280 mm section · 1,520 mm assembled length · half-depth seat
A dovetail drops into its socket while the projecting seat supports the overlapping timber.
Assembly: Lower the dovetailed member vertically onto the lap seat.

02 Hirahozo / Mortise & tenon
https://www.aisf.or.jp/~jaanus/deta/h/hirahozo.htm
280 × 320 mm post · 260 × 280 mm rail · 100 × 160 mm tenon
A rectangular tenon and four shoulders locate a rail against a mortised upright.
Assembly: Slide the tenon horizontally through the mortise until all four shoulders seat.

03 Aikaki-tsugi / Corner half-lap
https://kakuritools.com/blogs/woodworking-guides/japanese-wood-joinery-hand-tools
240 × 240 mm rails · 120 mm rebates · 840 mm lengths
Two half-depth rebates meet at a corner, preserving the full assembled thickness.
Assembly: Lower the upper rail vertically until the two rebates meet.

04 Kanawa-tsugi / Pinned oblique scarf
https://www.aisf.or.jp/~jaanus/deta/k/kanawatsugi.htm
320 mm deep × 320 mm wide · 1,200 mm scarf · 60 mm engagement
Oblique cheeks, blind end tongues and a separate central pin create a joint that must engage before it can lock.
Assembly: Bring the cheeks together · draw the tongues into their pockets · insert the central pin.

05 Okkake daisen-tsugi / Twin-pinned scarf
https://www.aisf.or.jp/~jaanus/deta/o/okkakedaisentsugi.htm
320 × 360 mm section · 1,100 mm stepped scarf · two 50 × 70 mm pins
Reversed stepped scarf faces enter from the side. Two independent pins then secure the joined beam.
Assembly: Slide sideways into the scarf · insert the first pin · insert the second.

06 Ōire mechigaitsuki ariotoshi / Housed blind dovetail
https://www.aisf.or.jp/~jaanus/deta/a/ariotoshi.htm
360 mm deep receiving beam · 40 mm housing · 180 mm deep dovetail
The beam shoulder enters a shallow housing while the upper dovetail seats in a deeper, half-blind pocket. The interlock is concealed from the side.
Assembly: Lower the dovetail into its pocket while seating the full-height shoulder.

07 Nuki · kusabi / Wedged crossing ties
https://www.aisf.or.jp/~jaanus/deta/n/nuki.htm
400 mm post · two 140 × 200 mm ties · 60 mm opposing wedge pairs
Two penetrating ties meet a post at separate levels. Opposing hardwood wedges close the gaps above the ties in four assembly stages.
Assembly: Insert the lower tie · insert the upper tie · seat the lower wedges · seat the upper wedges.

08 Koshikake kama-tsugi / Gooseneck lap splice
https://www.aisf.or.jp/~jaanus/deta/k/kamatsugi.htm
320 mm square section · 380 mm head and neck · half-depth seat
A narrow neck leads to a broad hooked head. The matching socket resists lengthwise separation while a lower seat supports the lap.
Assembly: Lower the gooseneck into its matching socket and seat the lap.

The JAANUS Kanawa illustration was inspected before constructing the scarf.
Additional comparison: photographed hand-made Kanawa and Kama models by Big
Sand Woodworking, https://www.bigsandwoodworking.com/product/joinery-models/
Andrew Hunter's public layout discussion was also consulted:
https://www.finewoodworking.com/2019/09/26/open-the-door-to-japanese-joinery
No source photographs or drawings are redistributed.

04 preserves oblique cheeks, central-depth blind end tongues, receiving
rabbets, and a central komisen. Its 60 mm pocket-entry allowance and dimensions
are an illustrative reconstruction, not a traced copy of a particular specimen.
05 interprets JAANUS's reversed oblique, housed and rabbeted scarf description;
the reference image was unavailable. Its explicit stepped profile is an
illustrative interpretation, not a measured tracing. Two pins enter in sequence.
06 follows variant 2 of the JAANUS ariotoshi entry. The upper dovetail is visible
from above, concealed from the side, and seated with a full-height shallow housing.
It is half-blind, not a joint concealed on every face.
07 combines two nuki at separate orthogonal levels in a single post. The overall
arrangement is illustrative; it is not a reconstruction of a named temple node.
Nuki and kusabi descriptions: https://www.aisf.or.jp/~jaanus/deta/k/kusabi.htm
The opposing wedges close a nominal gap. Timber compression, wedge preload,
friction, insertion force and swelling are not simulated.
08 includes a gooseneck with a lap seat. It does not include the extra L-shaped
blind tenons of the mechigai variant. This distinction is intentional.
The first three simpler studies remain as introductory comparisons.


09 Shihō kama-tsugi / The four-faced interlock
https://www.aisf.or.jp/~jaanus/deta/s/shihouari.htm
480 mm square post · 1,300 mm tall · 420 mm interlocking zone
Goosenecks appear on all four faces. Their hidden cutting direction runs across the diagonal of the post, allowing the two undercut halves to slide past one another.
Assembly: Follow the diagonal, then orbit the seated joint to read the four faces.
Interpretation: Illustrative diagonal-prismatic gooseneck profile clipped to a square post. JAANUS shihouari describes the common diagonal construction and its shihoukama variant.

10 Kai-no-kuchi · blind tongues / Concealed shell-mouth
https://www.bigsandwoodworking.com/kai-no-kuchi-splicing-joint/
480 mm square · 600 mm cheeks · four 45 mm blind tongues · 4 mm central relief
Four long triangular cheeks exchange places around the post. At both ends, small integral tongues disappear into blind pockets in the opposite shoulder.
Assembly: Open the post to find the four hidden tongues. Lower it again and watch the seams close.
Interpretation: Independent dimensional interpretation of Jon / Big Sand Woodworking blind kai-no-kuchi photographs (DSCF9207, 9205, 9204 and 1801). Triangular stub tongues, blind pockets and opposing triangular cheeks. Deliberate 4 mm central relief and inset tongue proportions, not a measured replica.

11 Kawai tsugite · geometric study / One cut, three directions
https://woodgears.ca/puzzles/3way_joint.html
480 mm cube · three cyclic orientations · tailored sector angles and central core (see notes)
Three interlocking fingers share a cubic centre. Reorient the moving member to form a straight splice or two different corners. A modern geometric study after Kawai’s invention.
Assembly: Separate before choosing an orientation. Each configuration closes along the same body diagonal.
Interpretation: Geometric construction after Matthias Wandel’s photographed six-sector cube demonstration; independent dimensions. Explicit kernel accommodation: sector boundaries offset by 0.4 degrees, alternating 59.2/60.8 degree sectors, plus a 4 mm radial central core assigned to the moving half. Halves are complementary, not identical. Threefold cyclic symmetry is preserved. Modern Kawai-inspired study, not a measured replica or attributed to ancient Japanese craft.

NEW REFERENCE INSPECTION
Shell-mouth: Jon / Big Sand Woodworking, photographed work (2024). Inspected
DSCF9207 (separate blind-tongue halves), DSCF9205 (engagement close-up) and DSCF1801
(assembly). Our triangular tongues and relief dimensions are independent choices.
Kawai: Matthias Wandel's original construction account, especially 3way18.jpg
and 3way24.jpg showing the six-sector cube and its complementary three fingers.
The original equal-sector construction failed in the current contact kernel.
kawai-kernel-repro.mjs/.json records a four-feature reproduction, with the exact
error 'Ambiguous near-coincident supports; no welding permitted'.
The delivered adaptation uses 0.4-degree boundary offsets, alternating 59.2 and
60.8-degree sectors, and a 4 mm radial central core allocated to the moving half.
This preserves threefold cyclic symmetry, but the halves are NOT identical.
These are deliberate, disclosed modifications, not a claim that the reference
was reproduced exactly. No third-party photos or drawings are redistributed.

12 Sanpō tome-tsugi / The locked mitred corner
https://suikoushya.com/2021/07/18/japanesejoinery-3waymiterjoint/
480 mm square stock · 1,280 mm arms · two 60 mm square integral upright tenons.
Reference: carpenter Takami Kawai's demonstration, video mOopMap5v6A.
Inspected separated rail at 0:15, joined rails at 0:24 and upright at 0:35.
Two crossed, staggered rail tongues engage horizontally before the upright's
twin tenons descend into blind mortises. Three compound mitres close the exterior.
Dimensions, tongue positions and cut depths are independent estimates. This is
a mechanism reconstruction, not a measured copy of the craftsman's specimen.
The final upper tongue is 10 mm below the first layout, avoiding a coincident
edge that the current display triangulator rejected. The final exact solids
validate; no display mesh replacement or kernel change was used.
Animation: rails engage over 0–52%; upright lowers over 52–100%. Reverse lifts
the upright completely before withdrawing the sliding rail.
